# Why Is There Frost or Ice on the Inside of My Windows? An Edmonton Winter Guide

Frost on the inside of your windows means warm, moist indoor air is touching glass or frames that are colder than its freezing dew point. In Edmonton winters, the cause is usually one of two things: too much humidity inside the house, or a window that has stopped insulating properly. The first is a quick fix. The second usually means the glass or the window needs attention.

Here's how to tell which one you're dealing with, and what to do about it.

## Why frost forms on the inside of windows

Warm air holds more moisture than cold air. When indoor air touches a cold surface, it cools down and drops that moisture as condensation. If the surface is below 0°C, the condensation freezes into frost or ice.

When it's -25°C outside, the inner pane of an older double-pane window can get cold enough for this to happen. A well-sealed, energy-efficient window keeps its inner pane much closer to room temperature, so frost rarely forms.

## Cause 1: Too much humidity inside the house

If frost appears on many windows at once, especially the bottom corners, high indoor humidity is the likely cause. Common sources:

- Cooking, showers and laundry without running the exhaust fan
- A humidifier set too high for the outdoor temperature
- Lots of houseplants, or a fish tank
- Drying clothes indoors
- A new home or recent renovation, where building materials are still drying out

In very cold weather, indoor humidity often needs to be lower than in fall. A humidity reading around 30% is a common winter target, and many homeowners go lower during deep cold snaps. Your furnace or HVAC technician can recommend the right level for your home.

**Quick fixes for humidity frost:**

- Run bathroom and kitchen fans for 15–20 minutes after showers and cooking
- Turn your humidifier down when the temperature drops
- Open blinds and curtains during the day so warm air reaches the glass
- Keep furniture away from windows and heat registers clear
- Use your HRV (heat recovery ventilator) if your home has one

## Cause 2: The window itself is failing

If frost forms on just one or two windows, or keeps coming back even after you lower humidity, the window is the problem. Signs to look for:

- **Frost on the frame or around the edges, not just the glass.** This points to air leaks or worn weatherstripping.
- **Fog or moisture between the panes.** This means the seal on the sealed glass unit has failed and the insulating gas has escaped. (We cover this in detail in our post on [condensation between window panes](/condensation-between-window-panes-edmonton).)
- **Drafts** you can feel near the window on a cold day.
- **Ice that won't melt** during the day, even with the blinds open.
- **Old single-pane or early double-pane windows**, often in homes built before the 1990s.

A failing window doesn't just frost up. It also wastes heat and can let moisture soak into sills, drywall and trim, leading to peeling paint, staining and mould.

## Humidity or failing window? A quick check

| What you see | Most likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Frost on most windows, bottom corners first | High indoor humidity | Lower humidity, run fans, open blinds |
| Frost on one or two windows only | Failing window or seal | Book an inspection |
| Fog or moisture between the panes | Failed glass seal | Replace the sealed glass unit |
| Frost around the frame edges | Air leaks, worn weatherstripping | Repair seals or replace the window |
| Water pooling on sills, peeling paint | Ongoing condensation | Fix the cause before damage spreads |

## FAQ

### Is frost on the inside of windows normal in Edmonton?

A little frost at the very edge of older windows during an extreme cold snap is common. Frost that covers the glass, returns daily, or shows up on only certain windows is a sign of high humidity or a failing window.

### Will frost on the inside of my windows damage my home?

It can. When frost melts, the water runs onto sills and into walls. Over a winter, that can rot wood, stain drywall and encourage mould.

### Do triple-pane windows stop frost?

They greatly reduce it, because the inner pane stays much warmer. Very high indoor humidity can still cause some condensation on any window.

### Can I just scrape the frost off?

You can wipe it away as it melts to protect sills, but that only treats the symptom. Lower your humidity or fix the window to stop it coming back.
